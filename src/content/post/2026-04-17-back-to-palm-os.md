Over the winter I was super into Palm Pilots, I even picked up a few
new ones and swapped the batteries. My current collection includes a
Tungsten E, Tungsten T3, Tungsten C and a Handspring Visor. I was daily
driving the C for quite awhile, but fell off when I upgraded my
phone.

![A Palm Pilot Tungsten C showing a list of food items](/content/images/back-to-palm-os/img_0428.jpeg)


A Palm Pilot Tungsten C showing a list of
food items

Well, today I’m going back! I recently [posted on
Mastodon](https://fosstodon.org/@alextheuxguy/116412041416969552) about my struggles trying to track calories. Everything on
the iOS store is enshittificated garbage. I shouldn’t have to pay a
monthly fee, see ads, have my data sold, create an account and agree to
ToS to track freaking calories.

![My Mastodon Rant](/content/images/back-to-palm-os/screenshot-from-2026-04-17-10-58-58.png)


My Mastodon Rant

Palm Pilots are from an era when software was allowed to be
complicated, and to that end there are a number of applications that
allow you to essentially create your own applications! Using [SmartList To Go](https://palmdb.net/search/smartlist), I was
able to create a database with a form for entering and tracking daily
calories. It’s not complex, but it doesn’t need to be.

![Screenshot showing a list of food items on Palm OS](/content/images/back-to-palm-os/screenshot0004.jpg)


Screenshot showing a list of food items
on Palm OS

I have a screen for seeing my daily log, sorted by meal then calorie
count. There’s a form for recording a new entry, and there’s a screen to
sum up all the calories in the day. I also keep a running log in each
entry of the daily calories (previous record total + current
calories).

![Screenshot showing a form to record a food item on Palm OS](/content/images/back-to-palm-os/screenshot0005.jpg)


Screenshot showing a form to record a
food item on Palm OS

I might expand the functionality by adding a second database that
remembers food items and their calories, then join it in with the log
view so I don’t have to lookup calories each time. The fact that it’s an
option to do that is awesome.

I also love that there’s the ability to build logical evaluators in
computed fields. Yeah, I had to download and read parts of the [136
page SmartList To Go 3.0 Handheld Reference Guide](https://thatalexguy.dev/files/smartlist_palm_os_guide.pdf), but why is that
such a bad thing? Why have we given up on making software that serves
our needs, and instead just let companies shovel subscription based crap
at us?

SmartList was **not** free back in the day (looks like
it was $50 USD, which is $84 today), but quality software shouldn’t have
to be free. What it should have to be is yours. Pay once, no ads, no
data collection, no online requirement. Someone that paid for SmartList
in 2005 can still get value out of it 21 years later.

Wish me luck as I restart my journey of weight loss, hopefully I can
shed some pounds and get faster on the bike! I’ll also be using the [Weight
Tracker application](https://github.com/devalexwhite/WeightTracker-PalmOS) I previously wrote for Palm OS.
